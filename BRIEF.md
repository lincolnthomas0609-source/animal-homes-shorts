# Project brief: How Animal Homes Work

**Promise to the viewer:** See inside an animal's home and discover how it works.

**Format:** Vertical 9:16 (1080×1920) 3D animated Shorts, 40–60 seconds, with narration, captions, and sound effects.

**What each episode does:** Starts with a specific question, surprising feature, or survival problem. Then it reveals the home through a cutaway, building it up or opening it piece by piece as the narration explains what each part does for the animal. Where the biology supports it, the story chains problems and features. Each solution reveals the next challenge, ending on the complete home and its most interesting function.

**Scope:** Animal homes only. Each episode makes clear which kind of structure it shows: a permanent home, a breeding nest, a temporary shelter, a borrowed shelter, or (labeled as such) a non-home structure like a food trap.

**Tone:** A visual discovery, not a lecture. Plain language for a general audience. Animals are never described as consciously designing or planning like human engineers. Say "the lodge has", "beavers pile", "this keeps", not "beavers designed" or "engineered".

**Accuracy rules:**
- Pick a specific species when the architecture depends on it, and name it on screen.
- Every narrated claim is checked against credible sources (wildlife agencies, museums, universities, peer-reviewed papers) before final narration. Links are saved with the script.
- No invented chambers, vents, escape routes, temperatures, dimensions, or behaviors. When sources disagree or homes vary, the narration says "often", "usually", or "some", and the visuals stay generic.
- Simplified or not-to-scale visuals get an on-screen "Illustrative" label.

**Visual signature (provisional until references are reviewed):**
- Each home sits inside a floating **diorama block** of its environment (soil, pond, snow, tree trunk) on a soft, plain studio background.
- A **section cut** sweeps down through the block to open it. Cut faces are a slightly different tone so cross-sections read instantly.
- Consistent camera grammar: exterior hook, then the cut, then a push into each feature, then a pull back to reveal the whole home.
- Clean, matte, simplified shapes; warm natural palette; simple stylized animals built from primitives.

**Production:** Blender 5.2 scripted in Python, rendered locally with EEVEE, assembled with ffmpeg. Narration from Kokoro (free, local) or user-supplied audio. Reusable components live in `blender/shorts/`.
