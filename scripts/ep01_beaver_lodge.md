# Ep 01: How a beaver lodge works

**Species:** North American beaver (*Castor canadensis*). On-screen label in the first shot.
**Structure type:** Permanent family home (lodge), contrasted at the end with the dam (water control, not a home).
**Target length:** about 50 s (147 words). Final timing comes from the narration track.
**Narration file:** `scripts/ep01_beaver_lodge.txt` (one line per caption beat)
**Scene script:** `blender/scenes/ep01_beaver_lodge.py` (to build after script approval)
**Status:** Draft script, fact-checked against the sources below. Waiting for your approval.

## Narration

> A beaver's front door is underwater. That's what keeps it safe.
>
> Beavers are slow on land, where most of their predators live. So the lodge sits in the pond, and the only way in is from below.
>
> But beavers breathe air. So the room isn't built. It's chewed out of a pile of sticks and mud, from underneath, with the floor just above the water.
>
> Before winter, they plaster the outside with mud, except near the top, leaving an air vent.
>
> When it freezes, that mud becomes a concrete-like shell that wolves can't break through.
>
> And there's usually a second tunnel: an escape route.
>
> The pond freezes over, but beavers don't hibernate. Their winter food is already stored underwater: a pile of branches, below the ice.
>
> And that wall downstream? It's not their home. It's the dam, keeping the water deep enough that the front door stays underwater.

| 9 | There's usually a second tunnel, an escape route | [HWW][hww] ("usually two underwater entrance tunnels, which allow for a means of escape if a predator enters"); [NPS GAAR][nps-gaar] ("usually two underwater tunnels") | Yes | Say "usually" as written. |
| 10 | Beavers don't hibernate | [Vermont F&W][vt] | Yes | |
| 11 | Winter food is stored underwater as a pile of branches, reachable below the ice | [NPS GAAR][nps-gaar]; [Vermont F&W][vt]; [Virginia Cooperative Extension][vtext]; [NPS Glacier][nps-glac] | Yes | The cache sits near the lodge. Glacier NPS notes it is placed deep, below the winter ice. |
| 12 | The dam keeps the water deep enough that the entrance stays underwater | [Virginia Cooperative Extension][vtext] ("impound and maintain water at a depth sufficient to cover the opening to the lodge"); [HWW][hww] (pond deep enough not to freeze to the bottom, year-round underwater access) | Yes | The dam is a separate structure, not the home. |

**Deliberately left out** because sources disagree or the detail isn't needed: the number of rooms inside (one per UMass; a feeding den plus a resting den per HWW and NPS), exact lodge dimensions (published "typical" sizes vary widely), and inside temperatures (only a commercial source gives a claim).

**Visual accuracy notes:**
- One chamber with a raised platform floor, two tunnels. Proportions are simplified, so show an "Illustrative" label during the cutaway.
- The dam goes downstream on the far side of the pond, with lower water beyond it.
