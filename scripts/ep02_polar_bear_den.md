## Narration v1 (55 s, 12 lines)

> A polar bear cub weighs about a pound at birth. It's born blind, in the middle of the Arctic winter. So its first home is a snowdrift.
>
> In autumn, a pregnant female digs a cave into deep snow, just big enough to turn around in. Then falling snow seals the entrance behind her.
>
> Snow traps heat. With her body warming the room, a den can be around forty degrees Fahrenheit warmer than outside.
>
> The tunnel often dips toward the entrance, so warm air stays up in the room. But a sealed room needs fresh air. So she often keeps a small air hole open.
>
> When the cubs arrive, she nurses them for months, without eating or drinking. In spring, she breaks out, and the cubs see sunlight for the first time.
>
> Here's the twist: only pregnant females spend the winter in a den. Every other polar bear stays active, all winter long.

**Visual accuracy notes:**
- Den proportions loosely follow Harington's averages: the entrance about 2 m long and 0.6 m wide, the room about 2 × 1.5 × 1 m. One room is shown; some dens have two or more.
- Newborn cubs are scaled at about 0.34 m (30–35 cm per PBI). They grow during the "months" line to suggest spring size, about 10–15 kg per PBI.
- Animals are stylized stand-ins. "Illustrative, not to scale" shows whenever the den is cut open.
