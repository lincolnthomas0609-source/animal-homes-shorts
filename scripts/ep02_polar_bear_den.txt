A polar bear cub weighs about a pound at birth.
It's born in the middle of the Arctic winter, inside a snowdrift.
In autumn, its mother digs a cave into deep snow, and falling snow seals her in.
Her body heat can keep the den around forty degrees Fahrenheit warmer than outside.
The tunnel often dips toward the entrance, so warm air stays in, and a small air hole often lets in fresh air.
For months, she nurses her cubs without eating or drinking.
In spring, the family digs out into the sunlight.
